Research

I look for geometric structure in learned representations that we can put to work in three ways: as a prior that makes learning better, as a verifier at inference time, and as a diagnostic that flags anomalies. Most of what I do sits in two lines of work.

World models and physical reasoning

Neural networks see the world through their representations. I ask what those representations encode about physics, and how to read it out without changing the model in the process.

  • Is physical plausibility written into the geometry of frozen image features? GeoPhys
  • Do real and generated videos trace different paths through a perceptual representation? ReStraV
  • When is a successful linear probe evidence of a linear representation? Linear probe
  • Does adapting a model to read out its physics change the physics it represents? Observer Effect
  • How do we keep video world models from losing what changes between frames? MotionJEPA
  • Can procedural data give vision transformers a better start? Procedural Core

Distributed learning

Many models, many owners, and data that never leaves home. I work on how such models can still learn together when their data barely overlap, and how to keep that collaboration small, fast and private.

  • How can federated training converge when the clients’ data barely overlap? FedLEx
  • Can a single round of communication be enough when every client sees a single class? FederatedFactory
  • How do we prune federated models without losing what each client knows? AutoFLIP
  • How do we keep collaborative optimisation private and secure? PPFLex

I grew up on Lake Iseo. The yellow walkway is for Christo and Jeanne-Claude’s The Floating Piers, which crossed the lake to Monte Isola in the summer of 2016.
