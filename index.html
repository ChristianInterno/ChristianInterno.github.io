I study how neural networks model the physical world, and how they learn together.

I am a researcher at Bielefeld University, working with Barbara Hammer; my PhD is funded by the Honda Research Institute Europe. After a Visiting Fellowship in Philip Torr’s Torr Vision Group at the University of Oxford, I continue to work with the group on world models.

My research focuses on world models and physical reasoning: I read the geometry of learned representations to find out what neural networks know about physics, and use it to tell whether a generated video could really happen. Broadly, I want physical plausibility to become something we can measure: principled ways to read physics out of a model, and to put that structure to work as a prior for learning, a verifier at inference time and a diagnostic for anomalies. Alongside this, I work on distributed learning, where many models learn together without sharing their data.

I have been a visiting researcher at Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory and Leiden University, and I studied data science at the University of Milano-Bicocca.

Research

World models and physical reasoning

Neural networks see the world through their representations. I ask what those representations encode about physics, and how to read it out without changing the model in the process.

The papers

Distributed learning

Many models, many owners, and data that never leaves home. I work on how such models can still learn together when their data barely overlap, and how to keep that collaboration small, fast and private.
